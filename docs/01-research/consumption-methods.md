# Consumption Methods for Broccoli Microgreens

**Phase:** 01 Research
**Status:** Complete
**Last Updated:** 2026-02-17

---

## Summary

Broccoli microgreens can be consumed in multiple forms. The central tradeoff is between **nutrient bioavailability** (maximized raw and fresh) and **convenience/palatability** (maximized as powder, capsules, or in masked applications like smoothies). Multiple product forms are viable and complementary. The device design should support the fresh consumption pathway primarily, while enabling optional powder production.

---

## Key Constraint: The Myrosinase Rule

Before evaluating any method, this rule governs all choices:

> **Sulforaphane is only produced when glucoraphanin (in the plant) contacts myrosinase (the enzyme, also in the plant).** This reaction requires plant cell damage — chewing, chopping, or blending. **Heat above ~60°C destroys myrosinase**, preventing this reaction. Any method that involves cooking the microgreens above 60°C dramatically reduces sulforaphane yield unless an external myrosinase source (e.g., mustard seed powder) is added.

Workaround: **Chop or blend first, then wait 40 minutes** before applying any heat. This allows the reaction to complete before myrosinase is destroyed.

---

## Method 1: Raw Fresh — Maximizes Nutrient Bioavailability

### Description
Consume directly from harvest. Eat as a garnish, in salads, in sandwiches/wraps, or as a standalone side dish.

### Bioavailability
- **Sulforaphane:** Maximum. Myrosinase fully active. Chewing is sufficient to trigger conversion.
- **Vitamins C, E, K:** Maximum. No heat degradation.
- **Minerals:** Fully available.

### Taste Profile
Broccoli microgreens have a mild, slightly peppery broccoli flavor — most people find it acceptable raw. The taste is milder than arugula microgreens and significantly less bitter than mature kale or broccoli.

### Practical Notes
- Best consumed immediately after harvest or within 5–7 days (refrigerated)
- Requires no preparation beyond harvesting and rinsing
- Most nutritionally complete method

### Convenience Rating: ★★★☆☆
Requires fresh greens on hand, brief rinsing, and integration into a meal. Fine for daily habit if the device is in the kitchen.

---

## Method 2: Smoothie — Best Balance of Nutrition and Palatability

### Description
Blend raw microgreens with fruit, liquid, and other ingredients. The broccoli flavor is almost entirely masked by fruit, especially banana and pineapple.

### Bioavailability
- **Sulforaphane:** Excellent. Blending ruptures cell walls, triggering the myrosinase reaction just as effectively as chewing. Consume immediately or within a few minutes of blending (sulforaphane degrades in solution over time).
- **Vitamins:** High. No heat applied.
- **Fiber:** Fully retained (unlike juicing).

### Taste Profile
Completely manageable. Effective flavor combinations:
- **Pineapple + coconut yogurt + broccoli microgreens** — tropical flavor, bright green
- **Banana + blueberry + oat milk + microgreens** — mild, sweet, masks greens entirely
- **Mango + spinach + microgreens + ginger** — tropical, spicy, very palatable
- Any smoothie with >100g banana will mask 30g of broccoli microgreens completely

### Practical Notes
- 30g microgreens in a smoothie is nearly undetectable by taste
- Consume within 15–20 minutes of blending for maximum sulforaphane
- Can freeze pre-portioned smoothie packs (fruit + measured microgreens) for grab-and-blend convenience
- Requires a blender

### Convenience Rating: ★★★★☆
The highest-nutrition high-convenience option. A morning smoothie habit integrates naturally with daily microgreen consumption.

## Method 3: Freeze-Dried Powder — Convenience Maximized

### Description
Freeze-dried microgreens are ground into a fine green powder. A daily dose (~1.5g powder = equivalent to 30g fresh) can be:
- Stirred into water, juice, or a glass of milk
- Mixed into yogurt
- Added to a smoothie
- Sprinkled on food (salads, avocado toast, eggs)

### Bioavailability
- **Sulforaphane:** Good, but reduced vs. fresh. GRN is well-preserved; myrosinase is partially active. To maximize SFN:
  - Add a pinch (1/8 tsp) of mustard seed powder as an external myrosinase source
  - Or hydrate the powder for 15–20 min before consuming
- **Vitamins:** ~90–95% of fresh (freeze-drying is the best preservation method)
- **Minerals:** Near 100%

### Physical Format
- **Size 0 capsule** holds ~500 mg powder
- **Size 00 capsule** holds ~700 mg powder
- 30g fresh dose → 1.5g freeze-dried powder → **2–3 size 0 capsules**
- 60g fresh dose → 3.0g freeze-dried powder → **4–6 size 0 capsules**
- This is a fully practical daily pill dose

### Taste
No taste when in capsules. Powder has a mild green/grassy flavor — easily masked in smoothies or juice.

### Convenience Rating: ★★★★★
Maximum convenience. Capsules can be taken anywhere, anytime, require no kitchen preparation, and have 12+ month shelf life. The trade-off is reduced sulforaphane bioavailability vs. fresh (20–30% reduction) unless mustard seed powder is co-administered.

**Commercial equivalents:** BeMicro Organic Broccoli Microgreens Powder, Revity Farms Freeze-Dried Powder, Silicon Valley Microgreens Powder — confirming this is a viable product form.

## Method 4: Dehydrated Chips — Tasty Snack, Lower Nutrition

### Description
Dry microgreens at low temperature (≤40°C), season, and consume as a snack. Alternatively, higher-temp dehydration creates crunchier chips.

### Process
1. Spread microgreens on dehydrator trays
2. Season with olive oil, lemon juice, tamari, or seasoning of choice
3. Dehydrate at ≤40°C for several hours until dry and crispy
4. Optional: sprinkle with toasted sesame seeds, nutritional yeast, or sea salt

### Bioavailability
- **Sulforaphane:** Reduced. Myrosinase activity diminished at ≤40°C; destroyed at higher temps.
  - Workaround: blend fresh first, wait 40 min, THEN dehydrate. This pre-converts GRN to SFN before heat is applied.
- **Vitamins:** ~60–80% of fresh at ≤40°C; much lower at higher temperatures
- **Minerals:** Near 100% retained

### Taste Profile
Surprisingly good. Broccoli microgreen chips seasoned with tamari + sesame are genuinely tasty. The drying concentrates flavor and creates a pleasing crunch. Comparable to kale chips.

### Convenience Rating: ★★★☆☆
Good as a snack product but requires preparation time and equipment. Not ideal for daily precise dosing.

### Design Note
If the device includes a low-temperature drying chamber (≤40°C), chips/crackers could be a secondary output product, using excess harvests.

---

## Method 5: Juiced — Not Recommended

### Description
Cold-press or centrifugal juicing to extract liquid from microgreens.

### Issues
- **Very low yield:** Microgreens are predominantly leaf and stem; juicing extracts minimal liquid and wastes significant biomass (the fiber and most nutrients remain in the pulp).
- **No practical benefit over smoothies:** A blender retains all fiber and is more nutritionally complete.
- **Not recommended** for broccoli microgreens.

---

## Method 6: Cooked — Not Recommended for SFN

### Description
Adding microgreens to hot soups, stir-fries, pasta, eggs, etc.

### Issues
- Cooking above 60°C destroys myrosinase, eliminating sulforaphane production from the GRN in the greens.
- **Workaround:** Chop microgreens and let sit 40 minutes BEFORE adding to hot food. This pre-activates sulforaphane before heat exposure.
- Alternatively, add a pinch of mustard seed powder to cooked dishes containing chopped microgreens.
- Vitamin C is also heat-labile; significant loss above 70°C.

### When Acceptable
If the user dislikes raw greens or smoothies and prefers cooked food, the workaround (pre-chop, 40-min wait, then cook) is a usable compromise, retaining most SFN while losing some heat-sensitive vitamins.

## Multiple Product Summary

The device could support several distinct "product forms" depending on the preservation system chosen:

| Product Form | Description | SFN Bioavailability | Convenience | Equipment |
|-------------|-------------|--------------------|-----------| ---------|
| **Fresh bunch** | Harvest and refrigerate | ★★★★★ | ★★★☆☆ | Refrigerator |
| **Fresh smoothie** | Blend raw with fruit | ★★★★★ | ★★★★☆ | Blender |
| **FD powder (loose)** | Mix into food/drink | ★★★★☆ | ★★★★☆ | Freeze dryer |
| **FD capsules** | Daily pill dose | ★★★★☆ | ★★★★★ | Freeze dryer + capsule filler |
| **Dehydrated chips** | Snack | ★★☆☆☆ | ★★★☆☆ | Dehydrator |
| **Dehydrated powder** | Add to food | ★★★☆☆ | ★★★★☆ | Dehydrator + grinder |

**Recommended daily routine for this device:**
1. Primary: morning smoothie with 30g fresh microgreens + fruit (maximum nutrition, high palatability)
2. Optional backup: freeze-dried powder capsules when fresh supply is low or for travel

## Sulforaphane Maximization Tips (Any Method)

1. **Chew thoroughly** (raw) or **blend well** (smoothie) — triggers myrosinase reaction
2. **Don't heat above 60°C** — destroys myrosinase
3. **Add mustard seed powder** (1/8 tsp) if using cooked or preserved forms — provides exogenous myrosinase
4. **Wait 40 minutes** after chopping before cooking — pre-converts GRN to SFN
5. **Consume fresh smoothies within 20 minutes** — SFN degrades in solution
6. **Eat daily** — sulforaphane does not accumulate; consistent exposure beats episodic mega-doses
