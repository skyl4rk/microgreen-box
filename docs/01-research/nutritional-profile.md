# Nutritional Profile of Broccoli Microgreens

**Phase:** 01 Research
**Status:** Complete
**Last Updated:** 2026-02-17

---

## Summary

Broccoli microgreens are among the most nutritionally dense foods studied. Their primary value lies in their exceptionally high concentration of glucoraphanin — the precursor to sulforaphane — combined with meaningful levels of vitamins C, E, and K, folate, and a mineral profile approximately 73% richer than mature broccoli florets. They are uniquely resource-efficient: they require 93–95% less water and produce equivalent nutrition to mature broccoli in a fraction of the space and time.

---

## Primary Bioactive: Glucoraphanin and Sulforaphane

Sulforaphane (SFN) is the compound most responsible for the exceptional health reputation of broccoli family foods. It is not present in the plant itself but is produced enzymatically when the plant tissue is damaged:

> **Glucoraphanin (GRN) + Myrosinase enzyme → Sulforaphane (SFN)**

This reaction is triggered by chewing, chopping, or blending.

### Concentrations in Broccoli Microgreens

From a 2023 human feeding study (PMC10606698):

| Compound | Concentration |
|----------|--------------|
| Glucoraphanin (GRN) | ~12.8–13.6 µmol/g fresh weight |
| Sulforaphane potential | ~6.4–7.2 µmol/g fresh weight |

**Key finding:** Broccoli microgreens contain GRN levels "similar in range to reported values for broccoli sprouts" — the gold standard for sulforaphane content. Microgreens are therefore a viable alternative to sprouts for sulforaphane delivery, with the advantage of being soil-grown (not water-sprouted) and having a slightly more complete vitamin profile.

### Comparison to Mature Broccoli

| Metric | Mature Broccoli | Broccoli Microgreens |
|--------|----------------|----------------------|
| Sulforaphane content | Baseline (1×) | 10–100× higher |
| Mineral content (avg) | Baseline | ~73% higher (compost-grown) |
| Growing time | ~90 days | 7–10 days |
| Water required | 158–236× more | Baseline |
| Land required | 93–95× more area for equivalent nutrition | Baseline |

Sources vary on the "10–100×" sulforaphane multiplier. The wide range reflects differences in growing conditions (light intensity, growing medium, age at harvest) and measurement methodology. A conservative and well-documented figure is **10–40× higher** than mature broccoli. The highest published figures (up to 100×) refer to optimal growing conditions compared to store-bought florets.

**Note:** Broccoli *sprouts* (3–5 days old) may contain slightly higher SFN than *microgreens* (7–14 days), but microgreens offer a more complete nutritional profile overall, and their GRN content is comparable to sprouts per the 2023 clinical study.

## Vitamins

| Vitamin | Amount per 100g fresh weight | % RDA (adult) | Notes |
|---------|------------------------------|---------------|-------|
| Vitamin C | ~117 mg | ~130% | Higher than mature broccoli (~89 mg/100g) |
| Vitamin E (α-tocopherol) | ~24.1 mg | ~160% | Notably elevated vs. mature plant |
| Vitamin K | ~130 µg | ~108–160% | Important for blood clotting, bone health |
| Folate (B9) | ~108 µg | ~27% | Cell regeneration, fetal development |
| Vitamin A (beta-carotene) | Present | — | Amount varies with light exposure |

**Key note on Vitamin E:** At 24.1 mg per 100g, broccoli microgreens provide 160% of the adult RDA for vitamin E in a single 100g serving. This is exceptional for a leafy green.

**Key note on light intensity and nutrient content:** Research shows that at higher PPFD (100–150 µmol/m²/s), chlorophyll, carotenoid, and anthocyanin content increases. At lower intensities (50–75 µmol/m²/s), ascorbic acid (vitamin C) and total phenolics are higher. This is a design consideration for the lighting system: the optimal spectrum depends on which nutrients are prioritized.

## Minerals

From the USDA-cited PMC study (PMC5362588) measuring compost-grown broccoli microgreens:

### Macrominerals (mg per gram fresh weight)
| Mineral | Amount | Notes |
|---------|--------|-------|
| Potassium (K) | 4.22 mg/g | Cardiovascular, muscle function |
| Phosphorus (P) | 0.76 mg/g | Bone, energy metabolism |
| Sodium (Na) | 0.66 mg/g | Fluid balance |
| Calcium (Ca) | 0.59 mg/g | Bone health |
| Magnesium (Mg) | 0.40 mg/g | 300+ enzymatic reactions |

### Microminerals (mg per gram dry weight)
| Mineral | Amount | Notes |
|---------|--------|-------|
| Iron (Fe) | 0.0125 mg/g DW | Oxygen transport |
| Manganese (Mn) | 0.00509 mg/g DW | Antioxidant enzyme co-factor |
| Zinc (Zn) | 0.00732 mg/g DW | Immune function, wound healing |
| Copper (Cu) | 0.000523 mg/g DW | Iron metabolism, antioxidant |

**Growing medium matters:** Compost-grown microgreens showed ~73% higher average mineral content than hydroponically grown counterparts (ratio 1.73 vs. 0.86–0.95 for hydroponics). This is a critical design decision for the growing system — soil/compost-based growing maximizes mineral density.

## Yield Data (Production Planning)

From the same PMC mineral study (PMC5362588), using 5"×5" trays (645 cm²):

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Fresh mass per 5"×5" tray | 20–24.6 g |
| Dry mass per 5"×5" tray | 1.53–1.96 g |
| Growing time | 7 days |
| Water used over full cycle | 65–90 mL total |

**Scaled to standard 10×20" (1,290 cm²) tray** (approximately 2× the 5"×5" tray):
- Fresh yield: ~80–170 g per tray (commercial growers report 140–325 g)
- Grow cycle: 7–10 days
- Seed required: ~13–25 g per 10×20 tray

The wide yield range reflects seeding density, growing medium, light, and water management differences. Commercial growers with optimized conditions achieve 200–325 g per standard tray.

## Other Phytonutrients

- **Glucosinolates** (beyond glucoraphanin): broccoli microgreens contain a full family of glucosinolates, each with distinct health effects.
- **Carotenoids:** Beta-carotene, lutein, zeaxanthin — amounts increase with higher light intensity.
- **Anthocyanins:** Present and increase under certain light conditions (higher PPFD).
- **Chlorophyll:** Present; increases with higher light intensity.
- **Phenolic compounds:** Total phenolics higher under lower light intensity.

## Effect of Growing Conditions on Nutrition

| Parameter | Effect on SFN/GRN | Effect on Vitamins | Notes |
|-----------|------------------|-------------------|-------|
| Light intensity (high, 100–150 µmol/m²/s PPFD) | Increases carotenoids, anthocyanins | Reduces vitamin C | More color, less C |
| Light intensity (low, 50–75 µmol/m²/s PPFD) | — | Increases vitamin C, phenolics | Paler but higher C |
| Growing medium (compost/soil) | — | — | ~73% more minerals than hydroponic |
| Illite mineral treatment during germination | +47% SFN | — | Emerging research, not practical for home |
| Harvest timing (day 7 vs day 14) | Highest GRN at early stages | — | Earlier = more GRN per gram |

**Design implication:** For a device optimized for sulforaphane, moderate-to-high PPFD (100–150 µmol/m²/s) and soil/compost growing medium is preferred. For maximum vitamin C, lower PPFD may be preferred. The device should default to a balanced intermediate setting.
